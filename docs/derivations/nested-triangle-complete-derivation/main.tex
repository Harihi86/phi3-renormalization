\noindent
This derivation concerns normalized scalar integrals with mass squared one.
The original graph symmetry factors, coupling powers and counterterm signs
are retained by the graph program; they are not redefined here.
It applies to the routed families L4E3G72, L4E3G73 and L4E3G250.
Scalar identities below have exact algebraic checks and independent numerical
checks. They do not by themselves certify a whole-graph quadrature result.

\section{Complete triangle integral}
For \(a,b>0\) and \(ab-(a+b-1)^2/4>0\), put
\[
 D=\{(x,y):x,y\geq0,\ x+y\leq1\},\qquad
 F=1+ax+by-ax^2-by^2+(1-a-b)xy .
\]
The three external invariants are \(a,b,1\). The unit invariant comes from
the physical external momentum in the original route. Define
\[
 {\cal L}(a,b)=\int_D\log F\,dx\,dy,\quad
 J(a,b)=\int_D\frac{dx\,dy}{F},\quad
 E(t)=2\sqrt{\frac{t+4}{t}}\asinh\frac{\sqrt t}{2}-2 .
\]
With
\[
 d=\frac{a+b-1}{2},\quad G=ab-d^2,\quad
 c_x=\frac{b(a-d)}{2G},\quad c_y=\frac{a(b-d)}{2G},\quad
 \Delta=1+\frac{ac_x+bc_y}{2},
\]
direct differentiation gives
\[
 (x-c_x)F_x+(y-c_y)F_y=2(F-\Delta).
\]
Apply the divergence theorem to
\((x-c_x,y-c_y)\log F\). The three oriented edge fluxes have constant
normal weights \(c_x,c_y,1-c_x-c_y\), respectively. Since
\(\operatorname{area}(D)=1/2\),
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 {\cal L}(a,b)=
 \frac{c_xE(b)+c_yE(a)+(1-c_x-c_y)E(1)}{2}
 -\frac12+\Delta J(a,b).}
\]
This fixes the boundary signs and the factor of one half.

\section{Rationalization and explicit dilogarithms}
Let
\[
 \alpha=(a-b)^2-2(a+b)+1=-4G<0,\quad
 \beta=2(a+b-1),\quad R(u)=\sqrt{5+\beta u+\alpha u^2},
\]
\[
 d(u)=1+u(a-b-1),\qquad e(u)=1-u(a-b+1).
\]
Integrating the second barycentric coordinate first gives
\[
 J=\int_0^1\frac{du}{R(u)}
 \log\frac{[R(u)+e(u)][R(u)+d(u)]}
 {[R(u)-e(u)][R(u)-d(u)]}.
\]
Every factor is positive because
\[
 R^2-d^2=4[1+bu(1-u)],\qquad
 R^2-e^2=4[1+au(1-u)].
\]
Set \(r=\sqrt5\) and \(z=(R-r)/u\). Then
\[
 u=\frac{\beta-2rz}{z^2-\alpha},\qquad
 \frac{du}{R}=-\frac{2\,dz}{z^2-\alpha},\qquad
 z_0=\frac{\beta}{2r},\quad
 z_1=\sqrt{(a-b)^2+4}-r .
\]
A stable expression for the negative interval width is
\[
 \delta=z_1-z_0=
 \frac{4r^2\alpha-\beta^2}
 {2r[2r\sqrt{(a-b)^2+4}+2r^2+\beta]}<0.
\]
For \(\ell\in\{a-b-1,-a+b-1\}\) and \(\sigma=\pm1\), define
\[
 N_{\ell,\sigma}(z)
 =(r+\sigma)(z^2-\alpha)
   +(\beta-2rz)(z+\sigma\ell).
\]
The leading coefficient is \(\sigma-r<0\). Its discriminant is
\(16b(b+4)\) for \(\ell=a-b-1\), and \(16a(a+4)\) for
\(\ell=-a+b-1\). Thus both roots \(\rho_{\ell,\sigma,j}\) are real,
and neither lies on \([z_1,z_0]\), because the corresponding factors
\(R+\sigma d\) or \(R+\sigma e\) stay positive.

Let \(\kappa=\sqrt{-\alpha}\) and
\[
 B=\frac{\delta}{z_0-i\kappa},\qquad
 A_{\ell,\sigma,j}=\frac{\delta}{z_0-\rho_{\ell,\sigma,j}}.
\]
Each \(A\) is real and \(1+A>0\). Introduce
\[
 {\cal P}(A,B)=
 \log(1-A/B)\log(1+B)
 -\Li\!\left(\frac{A(1+B)}{A-B}\right)
 +\Li\!\left(\frac{A}{A-B}\right).
\]
Then the complete inverse triangle is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 J=-\frac{2}{\kappa}\operatorname{Im}
 \left[
 2\log\frac{r+1}{r-1}\log(1+B)
 +\sum_{\ell}\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\sum_{j=1}^2
       \sigma\,{\cal P}(A_{\ell,\sigma,j},B)
 \right].}
\]
This is the conjugate-pole partial fraction identity, not a numerical
operation that discards an unexplained imaginary remainder.

\section{Principal-branch check}
For real \(A\), \(1+A>0\), and \(\operatorname{Im}B\ne0\),
\[
 {\cal P}(A,B)=\int_0^1
 \frac{B\log(1+Av)}{1+Bv}\,dv.
\]
Indeed, put \(Z(v)=A(1+Bv)/(A-B)\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{Im}Z(v)=
 \frac{A\,\operatorname{Im}B\,(1+Av)}{|A-B|^2}.
\]
For \(A\ne0\), this has fixed nonzero sign, so the segment avoids the
dilogarithm cut \([1,\infty)\). Moreover
\[
 (1-A/B)(1-Z(v))=1+Av>0.
\]
The two factors have opposite principal arguments. Their logarithms
therefore add to the real logarithm on the right. Differentiating the
displayed dilogarithm expression proves the identity. The removable case
\(A=0\) has \({\cal P}=0\).

\section{Integrated subtraction and angular control}
When physical external momenta are set to zero, the triangle invariants
are \((t,t,0)\), where \(t\) is the retained host loop momentum squared.
Thus the subtracted triangle factor uses
\[
 T_0=-\frac{E(t)}2,\qquad T=-{\cal L}(a,b).
\]
In particular, the host loop momentum is not set to zero by this operation.
The complete forest combination keeps
\[
 (H-1)K_0T_0+
 H\bigl[(K-K_0)T_0+K_0(T-T_0)+(K-K_0)(T-T_0)\bigr],
\]
with the original host propagator ratio \(H\) and parent factor \(K\).

All triangle coordinates in the quadratic angular control can also be
integrated exactly. Define
\[
 J_0=\frac{4\asinh(\sqrt t/2)}{\sqrt{t(t+4)}},\qquad
 J_2=\frac{2(1+J_0)}{t+4}.
\]
The needed moments are
\[
 U_1=-\frac{1-J_0}{2t},\quad
 C_{\rm pair}=-\frac{1-J_0}{4t},\quad
 C_{\rm fixed}=-\frac{J_0-3(1-J_0)/t}{12},\quad
 I_2=\frac{1-2J_0+J_2}{t^2}.
\]
Independent reference expressions follow from
\(E'(t)=(1-J_0)/t\) and \(I_2=-E''(t)\).
The control remains an exactly zero-mean angular term. It introduces
no physical counterterm. Absolute integrability is inherited from the
previous full-density bound by exact marginalization.

\section{Implementation and evidence}
The resulting full density has five coordinates: the host radius, two
angular coordinates and two parent-shape coordinates. The implementation
also retains an optional parent-matched radial map
\[
 w=\frac{hs}{1-s+hs},\qquad
 \frac{dw}{ds}=\frac{h}{(1-s+hs)^2},\qquad
 h=\ell_{\rm parent}^{-1/2}.
\]
This is an exact full-axis change of variables.

The scalar proof checks the divergence and rationalization identities,
all four discriminants and the branch identity. Eight cases, including
tiny, large and nearly degenerate invariants, agree with sixteen
independent 160-digit direct integrals to ninety relative digits.

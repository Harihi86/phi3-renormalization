\subsection{Different methods and different error objects}
The production exact-$A$ triangle uses standard-library real dilogarithms
or optional NumPy evaluations of the same kernel. A separate SciPy implementation
uses \texttt{spence}, adaptive QUADPACK logarithmic moments, and different
endpoint coordinates. Direct adaptive integration of the original paired
$A$ density checks the analytic primitive at fixed remaining fractions.
A further independent massless shape representation calculates the
constant and linear coefficient, and the preceding explicit bound controls
its finite-mass truncation. The inserted-zero-leg shape additionally has an
independent one-dimensional momentum representation evaluated with mpmath.
Thus matching two implementations of the same finite kernel is not the sole
check at extreme scales.

For bubbles, an 80-digit implementation evaluates the original elementary
one-loop bubble and angular averages, using adaptive QUADPACK rather than
production Gaussian panels or the positive Taylor-remainder rearrangement.
At $S/M=10^{308}$ the two-point comparison differs by about
$2\times10^{-12}$ and the symmetric three-point comparison by about
$1.5\times10^{-11}$. The overlap has an independent scale-free shape
constant computed with SciPy nodes and different endpoint maps; its final
$J/s$ differs by about $7\times10^{-11}$. Its explicitly bounded finite-mass
remainder is negligible at that scale. These coefficient/node differences
remain numerical diagnostics. The analytic radial-tail and finite-mass
remainder bounds have their separately stated, narrower meanings.

\subsection{Full original-graph pySecDec checks in this finite BPH scheme}
The official pySecDec 1.6.6 source was actually used to generate and compile
two original six-edge graphs: the primitive vertex and the subdivergent
nested triangle. Actual native Qmc runs were made at common ratios
$10^{12},10^{30},10^{100},10^{308}$, with an independent-seed repeat at the
largest ratio and an additional asymmetric triangle point. This is not a
claim that every topology has been separately integrated by that backend.

Let $\mu=1/\lambda$, $L=\log\lambda$, $c_{\rm off}(\mu)$ be the bare finite
Laurent coefficient of the normalized off-shell graph, and $c_0$ its
zero-point finite coefficient at $M=1$. The primitive graph has exact
residue $1/4$, and
\[
 J_{\rm primitive}=c_{\rm off}(\mu)-c_0-\tfrac12 L.
\]
For the nested triangle start directly from
$C_\gamma=-\Gamma(\epsilon)M^{-\epsilon}/2$,
$\bar R=I+C_\gamma T_{\rm outer}$ and $R=\bar R(s)-\bar R(0)$.
The zero-point bare poles are
\[
 \frac1{8\epsilon^2}
 +\frac{1/16-\gamma_E/4}{\epsilon}.
\]
Put $a_r(\mu)=\int_\Delta\log^r(\mu+W)$, evaluated independently with
adaptive SciPy. Exact regulated recursion and pole scaling then give
\[
 J_{\rm triangle}=\frac{L^2}{8}
 +\left(\frac{a_1}2-\frac18\right)L
 +c_{\rm off}(\mu)-c_0-\gamma_Ea_1-\frac{a_2}4.
\]
The exact pole coefficients prevent a stochastic pole error from being
multiplied by $L^2$. All finite Taylor terms are retained. Neither bare MS
poles nor a bare finite coefficient alone is called a BPH amplitude.
Recorded native triangle comparisons differ from the new core by at most
about $2.2\times10^{-9}$, consistent with reported stochastic uncertainties;
the primitive high-scale comparison also exposes and verifies the repair of
the previous endpoint-quadrature bias. All original series, seeds, library
hashes and input metadata are retained. A cached-native comparison explicitly
keeps the original execution date and identifies the later core comparison;
it does not claim a fresh upstream execution on every rebuild.

\subsection{Reproduction and primary sources}
Core computations use Python's standard library. NumPy, SciPy, mpmath and
full pySecDec are optional independent verification tools. Exact commands,
versions and evidence freshness are in the software report and JSON records.
\begin{itemize}
\item G. Heinrich et al., \emph{Numerical Scattering Amplitudes with pySecDec},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19768}.
\item Official executed release:
\url{https://github.com/secdec-research/secdec/releases/tag/v1.6.6}.
\item SciPy adaptive integration and dilogarithm definitions:
\url{https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.integrate.quad.html},
\url{https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.special.spence.html}.
\end{itemize}
The new algebra and implementation were produced in this collaboration.
A student should independently reproduce the relevant derivations before
reporting them as personally completed work. No general arbitrary-loop BPH
theorem or universal certified quadrature-error enclosure follows from these
finite computational checks.
